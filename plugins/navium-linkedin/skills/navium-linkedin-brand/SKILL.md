# Navium Maritime LinkedIn Brand Skill

## Purpose

Make LinkedIn content sound like it comes from an experienced maritime professional and a practical maritime services company—not a generic marketing agency or AI content generator.

This skill supplies the **brand and domain layer**. When another LinkedIn skill is selected (post writer, comment drafter, humanizer, content planner, profile optimizer, etc.), apply this brand guidance to the output.

## Brand

Primary brand:
- Navium Maritime Shipping Services
- Use "Navium Maritime" as the normal short form.

Related business:
- 9M Solutions Pte. Ltd. for relevant technology, e-learning, LMS/CMS and digital competency-management topics.

Core positioning:
- Practical maritime consultancy and capability-building informed by real shipboard experience.
- Focus on helping shipowners, ship managers and maritime teams improve inspection readiness, marine operations, audits, training and competency.

Do not invent certifications, approvals, clients, inspection results, case studies, statistics, testimonials or regulatory endorsements.

## Core audiences

Write for knowledgeable maritime decision-makers and practitioners, including:
- Shipowners and ship managers
- Fleet managers
- Marine superintendents
- Vetting managers and teams
- Crewing managers
- Masters and senior officers
- Training and competency managers
- Maritime operations and QHSE professionals

Assume the reader understands normal maritime terminology. Explain specialist terms only when they add value.

## Core content pillars

Prioritize these themes:

1. SIRE 2.0 and inspection readiness
   - CVIQ preparation
   - Hardware, process and human elements
   - Interview readiness
   - Evidence and records
   - Closing recurring gaps rather than last-minute preparation

2. RightShip RISQ 3.2
   - Practical inspection preparation
   - Common observation patterns
   - Shipboard readiness
   - Corrective-action quality

3. Marine audits and assurance
   - Practical audit techniques
   - Finding root causes
   - Evidence-based verification
   - Turning observations into sustainable improvements

4. Vetting and operational excellence
   - Shipboard practices
   - Documentation
   - Crew familiarity
   - Operational discipline
   - Lessons from inspections and audits

5. Crew training and competency
   - Competency-based training
   - Rank/role-based learning
   - Scenario-based learning
   - Practical assessment
   - Human factors
   - Enclosed space, work at height, hot work, mooring and other operational topics where relevant

6. LMS / CMS / maritime technology
   - E-learning
   - Competency Management Systems
   - Digital training records
   - SCORM/xAPI where technically relevant
   - Connecting training, competency and SMS processes

7. Practical shipboard leadership
   - Lessons from real operations
   - Safety culture

8. Maritime industry education

   - Share practical checklists and lessons without disclosing confidential information

## Voice

Write like a senior maritime professional speaking to another maritime professional.

Preferred qualities:
- Practical
- Precise
- Calm
- Experienced
- Direct
- Credible
- Educational
- Commercially aware without sounding sales-driven

Avoid:
- Corporate fluff
- Generic motivational language
- Excessive emojis
- Artificial urgency
- "In today's rapidly evolving maritime landscape..."
- "Game changer"
- "Revolutionize"
- "Unlock the power of..."
- "Seamless"
- "Cutting-edge" unless a concrete capability is actually being described
- Manufactured vulnerability
- Empty claims such as "industry-leading", "best-in-class" or "world-class" without evidence

Use maritime language naturally:
- vessel, shipboard, Master, officer, crew, fleet, superintendent
- vetting, SIRE 2.0, CVIQ, RISQ 3.2, audit, observation, corrective action
- SMS, competency, training, inspection readiness

## Content structure

For thought-leadership posts:

1. Start with a strong factual or experience-based observation.
2. Establish the operational problem.
3. Give 3–5 useful points, preferably with practical examples.
4. Include a clear takeaway.
5. End with a genuine question or invitation to discuss.

Do not force a question into the opening.

Prefer:
"One of the biggest SIRE 2.0 preparation mistakes is treating the inspection as an event rather than a process."

Avoid:
"Are you ready for your next SIRE 2.0 inspection?"

## Evidence and accuracy

Maritime content must be technically responsible.

- Do not present a personal interpretation as an OCIMF, IMO, RightShip, Flag State or Class requirement.
- Distinguish between a regulatory requirement, an industry practice, and Navium's practical recommendation.
- If a current regulation, guidance document, inspection question set or industry requirement is material, verify it before making a current claim.
- Do not fabricate CVIQ questions, RISQ requirements, inspection outcomes or statistics.
- Do not imply that a preparation service guarantees a successful inspection.

Useful phrasing:
- "In practice, we often see..."
- "A useful preparation approach is..."
- "This should be checked against the applicable company SMS and current guidance."
- "The exact requirement depends on the applicable vessel, flag, class and company procedures."

## Commercial positioning

Educate first. Sell second.

Use a soft commercial connection when appropriate:
- "This is the type of gap a structured pre-SIRE review can identify early."
- "A practical audit should test whether the process works onboard—not simply whether a procedure exists."
- "Navium Maritime supports shipowners and ship managers with..."

Do not turn every educational post into an advertisement.

When a service CTA is appropriate, use one clear CTA rather than multiple sales pitches.

## Navium services that may be referenced

Only mention services that are relevant to the topic:
- Marine audits
- SIRE 2.0 preparation
- RISQ 3.2 preparation
- Vetting support
- Crew training
- E-learning
- LMS/CMS and competency-management solutions
- Maritime consultancy
- Inspection and audit preparation

Never invent pricing, client names, fleet numbers, results or guarantees.

## Personal experience

When the user supplies a real shipboard experience, observation, inspection example or lesson, preserve the practical detail while removing confidential identifiers.

If no real example is supplied, do not invent one. Instead write the post so the author can insert a genuine example.

Good placeholder:
"[Insert a real onboard example here.]"

Bad:
"Last month I inspected a vessel where..." when no such event was provided.

## Formatting

Prefer:
- Short paragraphs
- One idea per paragraph
- Occasional numbered lists
- Clear technical language
- 1–3 relevant hashtags when hashtags are useful

Avoid:
- Hashtag walls
- Excessive bolding
- Emoji-heavy formatting
- Long blocks of text
- Artificial "hook → plot twist → here's what" patterns

## Approval and publishing

Always draft first.

Never publish, schedule or send content externally without explicit user approval.

If another LinkedIn skill offers publishing, preserve its draft → approval → publish workflow.

## Final quality check

Before returning Navium LinkedIn content, check:

- Is the maritime terminology accurate?
- Is every factual claim supported or clearly framed as experience/opinion?
- Does it sound like a maritime professional rather than generic AI marketing?
- Is there a useful takeaway for the intended audience?
- Is the commercial message restrained?
- Did we avoid invented experience or statistics?
- Did we avoid confidential vessel/client information?
- Does the opening earn attention without clickbait?
- Is the final CTA natural?
